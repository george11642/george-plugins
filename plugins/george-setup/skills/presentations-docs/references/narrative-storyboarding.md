# Narrative Storyboarding for Presentations

The storyboard is built BEFORE slides. It maps the story logic, emotional beats, and visual flow of the presentation. Designing slides without a storyboard produces disjointed, hard-to-follow talks.

---

## Why Storyboard First

Building slides before the narrative is like building rooms before the floorplan. Common symptoms of skipping storyboarding:

- Slides that contradict each other's emphasis
- Audiences confused about what the presenter is trying to say
- Too many slides with no clear climax
- "Data dump" structure: facts without argument
- The presenter spends half the talk apologizing for "one more slide"

Storyboarding takes 20-30 minutes and saves hours of slide revision. The hardest part — figuring out what to say and in what order — is solved before a single slide exists.

---

## Storyboard Framework

### Step 1: Define the Core Message

Write ONE sentence that captures the entire talk's point:

```
"[Audience] should [believe/do/know] [core claim] because [evidence]."
```

Examples:
- "The grant committee should fund our trial because our Phase I data shows a 3× efficacy improvement over current standard of care."
- "The engineering team should adopt the new architecture because it reduces incident response time by 80% with minimal migration cost."
- "The board should approve the Series B because we've achieved product-market fit in three segments and are capital-constrained for the next growth phase."

If you cannot write this sentence, you are not ready to storyboard. Clarify your message first.

### Step 2: Map the Story Beats

Lay out index cards, sticky notes, or a text list — one beat per card. Each beat answers ONE question from the audience.

Beat template:
```
SLIDE N: [Question this slide answers]
- Content: [What goes on slide]
- Visual: [What the main visual is]
- Key message: [One-sentence takeaway]
- Transition to next: [Bridge sentence]
```

### Step 3: Check the Arc

The beats should follow the narrative arc template (see below). Rearrange cards until the arc is clear and each beat flows logically into the next.

### Step 4: Assign Timing

Every beat gets a time budget. The presentation should be timed on the storyboard, not after building slides.

### Step 5: Lock Before Building

Do not open PowerPoint/Keynote until the storyboard is locked. Changes to the narrative after slides are built cost 3-5× more time than changes at the storyboard stage.

## Narrative Arc Template

This arc works for scientific talks, business pitches, product demos, and academic lectures. Adjust section proportions to fit your total slide count and time.

### Distribution Table (for 45-minute talk)

| Section | % of Talk | # Slides (40 total) | Minutes |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|---------|
| Opening | 5-10% | 2-4 | 2-4 min |
| Context | 10-15% | 4-6 | 5-7 min |
| Problem | 5-10% | 2-4 | 2-4 min |
| Approach | 15-25% | 6-10 | 7-11 min |
| Results | 40-50% | 16-20 | 18-22 min |
| Implications | 15-20% | 6-8 | 7-9 min |
| Closure | 2-5% | 1-2 | 1-2 min |

For a 15-minute talk (12-15 slides), scale proportionally:
- Opening: 1 slide
- Context: 1-2 slides
- Problem: 1 slide
- Approach: 2-3 slides
- Results: 4-6 slides
- Implications: 1-2 slides
- Closure: 1 slide

### Section 1: Opening (5-10%)

Purpose: Establish a hook. Make the audience care before explaining anything.

**Types of opening hooks:**
- **Surprising statistic**: A number that contradicts expectations
- **Rhetorical question**: "What if the treatment everyone uses is actually making it worse?"
- **Visual challenge**: Show a striking image, ask the audience to interpret it
- **Narrative anecdote**: Brief story that makes the abstract concrete
- **Vivid contrast**: Before/after, then/now, us/them

**Opening slide beats:**
1. Hook slide — single powerful image or number
2. Stakes slide — "Why this matters to you/the world"

What NOT to do in the opening:
- Do NOT start with "Thank you for having me"
- Do NOT start with an outline slide
- Do NOT start with a title card and then immediately discuss methods
- Do NOT open with a dense chart — earn it first

### Section 2: Context (10-15%)

Purpose: Give the audience enough background to understand the problem. No more.

**Context slide beats:**
- State of the field: what is known and established
- Key frameworks or definitions the audience needs
- Why now: what changed that makes this timely

Rule: Include only context that is required to understand the problem. Everything else is either background slides for questions or should be cut.

### Section 3: Problem (5-10%)

Purpose: Create intellectual tension. Name the specific gap, question, or challenge.

**Problem slide beats:**
- The specific gap: "We know X, but we don't know Y"
- Why the gap matters: consequences of not solving it
- Your research question / hypothesis / proposal (stated once, clearly)

The problem section should feel uncomfortable. The audience should feel the absence of the answer. If there's no tension here, the opening lacked sufficient stakes.

### Section 4: Approach (15-25%)

Purpose: Explain HOW you addressed the problem. Build credibility.

**Approach slide beats:**
- Overall strategy (one overview slide — a flowchart or diagram, not bullet points)
- Key methodological decisions (1 slide per major decision, maximum)
- Validation or pilot data (if strengthens credibility)
- Limitations acknowledged proactively (builds trust; do not hide limitations)

Rule: The approach should be just long enough to make the results believable. If the audience doesn't trust the method, the results are worthless. If the approach is over-explained, the audience loses the thread.

### Section 5: Results (40-50% — the longest section)

Purpose: Deliver the answer to the question you raised in Section 3.

**This is where most presentations are too thin.** Spend the most time here.

**Results section structure:**
1. **Lead result slide**: Your most important finding. One chart. One number. Full visual.
2. **Supporting result slides**: Evidence that the lead result is robust (controls, replications, secondary endpoints)
3. **Unexpected findings**: Any surprising outcomes (increases credibility)
4. **Comparison slide**: How your results compare to the prior state of the art or competition

**Result slide format:**
- One chart per slide
- Title = the finding, not the description ("Treatment reduces mortality 3×" not "Mortality comparison between groups")
- Data takes 60-70% of slide area
- Direct labels on all data points
- One-sentence callout: the implication in plain language

### Section 6: Implications (15-20%)

Purpose: Tell the audience what to THINK and DO with the results.

**Implications slide beats:**
- Scientific/business significance: why this result changes something
- Broader impact: who else is affected beyond your immediate audience
- Next steps: what you or others should do next (specific, not vague)
- Open questions: what still needs to be solved (shows depth and honesty)

Distinguish between:
- What the data shows (results)
- What the data means (implications)

Do NOT conflate them. The results section is about facts. The implications section is about judgment.

### Section 7: Closure (2-5%)

Purpose: End with direction, not a whimper.

**Closure slide beats:**
- Summary: three key takeaways maximum (one slide, very brief)
- Call to action: ONE specific thing you want the audience to do/decide/think

What NOT to do in closure:
- Do NOT end with "Questions?" as the final slide
- Do NOT summarize everything — summarize the single most important thing
- Do NOT introduce new information in the closure
- Do NOT end with a transition slide; end with your call to action slide left visible during Q&A

## Slide-Level Story Beats

Each individual slide should pass this test:

**The one-question test**: What single question does this slide answer?
- If the answer is more than one question: split the slide
- If the answer is "nothing, it's a transition": remove the slide

**Beat types and their functions:**

| Beat Type | Function | Visual Convention |
|-----------|----------|------------------|
| Claim slide | Assert a key point | Oversized title, minimal body |
| Evidence slide | Support a claim | Chart or data dominates |
| Comparison slide | Show difference | Side-by-side or before/after |
| Process slide | Explain a sequence | Flowchart or numbered steps |
| Definition slide | Establish shared vocabulary | Text-heavy acceptable here |
| Impact slide | Show real-world consequence | Photo or compelling visual |
| Summary slide | Recap key takeaways | 3 bullets maximum |
| Transition slide | Signal section change | Large section title, minimal content |

## Timing Distribution for 45-Minute Talk

Planned time vs. typical actual time:

| Section | Planned | Typical Actual | Risk |
|---------|---------|----------------|------|
| Opening | 3 min | 4-5 min | Over-expanded context |
| Context | 6 min | 8-10 min | Getting questions early |
| Problem | 3 min | 3-4 min | Low risk |
| Approach | 9 min | 10-12 min | Questions on methods |
| Results | 18 min | 15-18 min | If Q&A interrupts |
| Implications | 7 min | 5-8 min | Often rushed |
| Closure | 2 min | 1-2 min | Often cut |
| Q&A Buffer | 7 min | 5-10 min | Depends on audience |

**Reserve 15% of allotted time for overruns and Q&A.** For a 45-minute slot: plan a 38-minute talk.

## Visual Storyboarding Checklist

Work through this before building slides:

### Narrative Checks
- [ ] Core message written in one sentence
- [ ] Each section has a clear purpose (opening/context/problem/approach/results/implications/closure)
- [ ] Results section is the longest section (40-50%)
- [ ] Every slide answers exactly one question
- [ ] No slide introduces information without using it later (or it's cut)
- [ ] Transitions between sections are explicit (transition slides or strong bridge sentences in notes)

### Visual Checks
- [ ] Every slide has a clear primary visual identified (not just text)
- [ ] No two adjacent slides have the same layout
- [ ] Charts are identified by type for each results slide
- [ ] At least one visual per results slide
- [ ] Opening slide has a striking visual anchor

### Pacing Checks
- [ ] Time budget assigned per section
- [ ] Total planned time is 85-90% of allotted time slot
- [ ] Results section has enough slides (underestimating is the most common error)
- [ ] No section is more than 25% of total slides except Results

### Layout Consistency
- [ ] Maximum 2 layout templates defined (standard + section divider)
- [ ] Color palette assigned and consistent across all planned slides
- [ ] No sudden layout changes between adjacent slides

## Narrative Review Before Building Slides

After completing the storyboard and before opening any slide tool:

### Read-Aloud Test

Read the core message of each beat aloud in order:
```
"This talk is about [core message].
 First, I'll establish [opening hook].
 The context is [context summary].
 The problem is [problem statement].
 My approach was [approach summary].
 The key finding is [lead result].
 This means [implications].
 So I'm asking you to [call to action]."
```

If this summary is coherent and compelling in 90 seconds, the storyboard is ready.

### The "So What?" Test

At each section transition, ask "So what?" from the audience's perspective:
- Opening → "So what? Why should I care?" → answered by Context
- Context → "So what? We already know this" → answered by Problem
- Problem → "So what? Can it be solved?" → answered by Approach
- Approach → "So what? Does it work?" → answered by Results
- Results → "So what? What do I do with this?" → answered by Implications

If any transition doesn't naturally raise and answer the "So what?", the narrative has a gap. Fix it in the storyboard, not in the slides.

### Anti-Patterns to Fix Before Building

| Anti-Pattern | Symptom | Fix |
|-------------|---------|-----|
| Data dump | Results section is a list of all data collected | Select only the 3-5 findings that directly answer your research question |
| Buried lead | Most important finding is slide 8 of 12 in results | Move lead result to first results slide |
| Weak opening | First slide is title card with no hook | Add hook slide before or replace title with hook |
| Method overload | Approach section is longer than results | Cut methods to minimum needed for results to be credible |
| Missing CTA | Presentation ends without a specific ask | Add one concrete call-to-action slide as the final visible slide |
| Split message | Core message sentence needed "and" | Split into two talks or cut one thread |
