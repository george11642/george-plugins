# Launch Playbook

## ORB Framework (Channel Strategy)

Structure all launch marketing across three channel types. Everything funnels to owned.

| Channel Type | Examples | Why It Matters |
|---|---|---|
| **Owned** | Email list, blog, podcast, community (Slack/Discord), website | Compounds over time, no algorithm risk, direct relationship |
| **Rented** | Social media, app stores, YouTube, Reddit | Speed but no stability; use to drive traffic to owned |
| **Borrowed** | Guest posts, podcast interviews, influencer partnerships, co-marketing | Instant credibility; convert borrowed attention into owned relationships |

### Starting Owned Channels
- Industry lacks quality content --> start a blog
- People want direct updates --> focus on email
- Engagement matters --> build a community

### Using Rented Channels
- Pick 1-2 where audience is active
- Twitter/X: threads that spark conversation --> link to newsletter
- LinkedIn: high-value posts --> lead to gated content or email signup
- Marketplaces: optimize listing --> drive to site

### Using Borrowed Channels
1. List industry leaders your audience follows
2. Pitch win-win collaborations
3. Use SparkToro or Listen Notes to find audience overlap
4. Set up affiliate/referral incentives

## Five-Phase Launch

### Phase 1: Internal Launch
- Recruit early users one-on-one to test free
- Collect feedback on usability gaps
- Validate core functionality with friendly users

### Phase 2: Alpha Launch
- Landing page with early access signup
- Announce product exists
- Invite users individually
- MVP working in production

### Phase 3: Beta Launch
- Work through early access list (some free, some paid)
- Marketing teasers about problems you solve
- Recruit friends, investors, influencers to test and share
- Add: coming soon page, "Beta" badge, email invites, early access toggle

### Phase 4: Early Access Launch
- Leak product details: screenshots, GIFs, demos
- Gather quantitative + qualitative data
- Run user research (incentivize with credits)
- Optionally run product/market fit survey
- Expansion: throttle invites in batches (5-10%) OR invite all under "early access" framing

### Phase 5: Full Launch
- Open self-serve signups
- Start charging
- Announce GA across all channels
- Touchpoints: customer emails, in-app popups, website banner, blog post, social posts, Product Hunt, BetaList, Hacker News

## Product Hunt Checklist

### Before Launch Day
- [ ] Build relationships with supporters and communities
- [ ] Optimize listing: compelling tagline, polished visuals, short demo video
- [ ] Study successful launches
- [ ] Engage in relevant communities (provide value before pitching)
- [ ] Prepare team for all-day engagement

### Launch Day
- [ ] Treat as all-day event
- [ ] Respond to every comment in real-time
- [ ] Answer questions and spark discussions
- [ ] Encourage existing audience to engage
- [ ] Direct traffic to site for signups

### After Launch Day
- [ ] Follow up with everyone who engaged
- [ ] Convert PH traffic into owned relationships (email)
- [ ] Continue momentum with post-launch content

## Post-Launch Playbook

### Immediate Actions
- [ ] Automated onboarding email sequence
- [ ] Include announcement in roundup email
- [ ] Publish competitor comparison pages
- [ ] Add feature sections across website
- [ ] Create interactive demo (e.g., Navattic)

### Ongoing Launch Strategy

**Major updates** (new features, overhauls): Full campaign -- blog, email, in-app, social
**Medium updates** (integrations, UI): Targeted -- email to segments, in-app banner
**Minor updates** (bug fixes, tweaks): Changelog and release notes

Tips:
- Stagger announcements to maintain momentum
- Reuse high-performing tactics
- Signal active development -- even small changelog updates build retention
