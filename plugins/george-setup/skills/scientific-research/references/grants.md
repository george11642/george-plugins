# Research Grants Reference

## Overview

Write competitive proposals for NSF, NIH, DOE, DARPA, and Taiwan NSTC. Grants are persuasive documents demonstrating scientific rigor, innovation, feasibility, and broader impact.

## Agency Comparison

| Feature | NSF | NIH | DOE | DARPA | NSTC |
|---------|-----|-----|-----|-------|------|
| Core criteria | Merit + Broader Impacts | Significance, Innovation, Approach | Technical merit + mission | High-risk/high-reward | Innovation + Feasibility |
| Page limit | 15 pages | 12 pages (R01) | Varies | Varies | CM03 form |
| Key emphasis | Education, diversity | Preliminary data, rigor | Energy/computation | Prototypes, demos | Bilingual abstract |

## Core Proposal Components

### 1. Executive Summary / Abstract
- Compelling hook establishing importance
- Clear problem statement, novel approach, expected outcomes
- Team qualifications and broader impact
- Length: NSF 1 page, NIH 30 lines, DOE ~1 page

### 2. Specific Aims (NIH) / Objectives
**NIH Specific Aims** (1 page):
- Opening: gap in knowledge + significance
- Long-term goal, immediate objectives, central hypothesis
- 2-4 specific aims (independent but complementary)
- Each aim: statement, rationale, working hypothesis, approach, expected outcomes
- Payoff paragraph

### 3. Research Strategy / Project Description
- Background and significance
- Innovation narrative
- Detailed methodology with preliminary data
- Timeline and milestones
- Potential problems and alternatives

### 4. Broader Impacts (NSF)
Address 1+ areas: teaching/training, underrepresented groups, infrastructure, dissemination, societal benefits. Be specific with concrete activities, timelines, and assessment measures.

### 5. Budget
- Personnel (salary + fringe), equipment (>$5K), travel, materials, indirect costs
- NIH: modular ($250K increments) for most R01s, salary cap ~$221,900
- NSF: up to 2 months summer salary
- DOE: often requires cost sharing
- DARPA: detailed by phase and task

## Review Criteria

**NSF**: Intellectual Merit (advance knowledge?) + Broader Impacts (benefit society?) -- equally weighted

**NIH** (scored 1-9): Significance, Investigator(s), Innovation, Approach, Environment

**DARPA**: Scientific/technical merit, contribution to mission, cost realism

**NSTC**: Innovation, Feasibility, PI Capability, Value

## Writing Principles

- Write for multiple audiences (specialists, related fields, program officers)
- Build narrative: Hook > Problem > Solution > Evidence > Impact > Team
- Use active voice, strong verbs, precise language
- Balance innovation with feasibility
- Include figures: conceptual diagrams, preliminary data, timelines

## Common Award Types

**NSF**: Standard ($500K/5yr), CAREER (early career), RAPID (urgent), EAGER (exploratory)
**NIH**: R01 (main), R21 (exploratory), R03 (small), K99/R00 (independence), F31/F32 (fellowships)
**DOE**: Office of Science, ARPA-E, EERE
**DARPA**: Program-specific BAAs, YFA (early career)

## Development Timeline

1. **Planning** (2-6 months out): Identify opportunity, assemble team, develop preliminary data
2. **Drafting** (2-3 months): Write aims first, then strategy, figures, budget
3. **Internal Review** (1-2 months): Colleague feedback, mock review
4. **Finalization** (2-4 weeks): Format, forms, biosketches, letters
5. **Submission** (1 week before): Upload, verify, submit 48h early

## Resubmission (NIH A1)

- 1-page introduction summarizing criticisms and changes
- Address every major criticism
- Strengthen weak areas with new data
- Respectful tone toward reviewers
- Use full 37-month window if needed

## Common Mistakes

- Not addressing review criteria explicitly
- Mismatch with agency mission
- Vague objectives
- Insufficient preliminary data
- Overly ambitious for timeline/budget
- Poor broader impacts (NSF)
- Exceeding page limits (automatic rejection)
- Submitting at deadline (portals crash)
